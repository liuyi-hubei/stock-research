# Jack Welch: Deep Distillation

## Source Unit

Primary source: DOC-057, an autobiography. It is retrospective and self-authored, so distinguish operating principles from reputation management and hindsight selection.

## First Principles

### Face reality early

Strategy starts with an unsentimental description of present competitive position, economics, people, and constraints. Plans built to satisfy hierarchy create delay and hidden losses.

### Candor increases organizational speed

Information must travel across levels and functions without being softened to protect status. Candor shortens the distance between a problem and a decision.

### People allocation is capital allocation

Selecting, developing, rewarding, and removing leaders determines whether strategy becomes operating results. Talent decisions should be differentiated rather than uniformly polite.

### Focus resources where the company can win

The “number one or number two” rule is a forcing device: confront weak competitive positions and stop subsidizing businesses without a credible route to advantage.

### Boundarylessness and learning beat hierarchy

Ideas should move across functions, businesses, ranks, and external organizations. Rank is not evidence; useful knowledge can come from anywhere.

### Quality is a customer and process system

Six Sigma is valuable when it reduces defects, variation, and customer pain through measurable process improvement. It becomes harmful when reduced to internal ceremony or credentialism.

## Causal Model

Reality-based diagnosis -> focused priorities -> strong people in accountable roles -> candid information flow -> rapid correction -> better customer outcomes and capital returns.

Bureaucracy -> filtered information and diffused accountability -> slow reaction -> resources trapped in weak businesses -> strategic decay.

## Operating Sequence

1. Describe current reality and competitive position without plan-language.
2. Choose a few businesses and capabilities where advantage is achievable.
3. Put the strongest suitable leaders in the critical roles.
4. Align incentives, responsibility, and authority.
5. Remove layers and procedures that exist mainly to preserve hierarchy.
6. Invite challenge and move ideas across organizational boundaries.
7. Translate strategy into measurable customer, quality, and operating outcomes.
8. Review frequently, surface bad news early, and reallocate people and capital.
9. Change before crisis removes the freedom to choose.

## Failure Modes

- Declaring a transformation without changing incentives or accountability.
- Using rankings mechanically and creating fear, politics, or short-term gaming.
- Treating acquisitions as a substitute for operating competence.
- Letting financial targets dominate customer and process reality.
- Turning Six Sigma into bureaucracy.
- Confusing charisma and decisiveness with accurate diagnosis.
- Retrospective narratives that understate luck, cycle, or harms to other stakeholders.

## Limits As An Investment Lens

Strong execution cannot repair a structurally poor business model or an excessive purchase price. Welch's framework is best used to assess management quality, organizational learning, and the credibility of strategic execution. It must be paired with cash-flow economics, competitive durability, and valuation.

## Compact Operating Algorithm

What is the unvarnished reality? Where can this organization genuinely win? Are the right people accountable for the right outcomes? Does information move fast enough to expose mistakes? Are quality programs improving customers or feeding bureaucracy? If the organization cannot answer these candidly, management quality is not yet proven.
